## Why everything ends up in one place

Databricks is usually adopted for the work it's best at: machine learning, streaming, complex large-scale processing. Then the simple nightly ETL lands there too, because that's where the data is. Then the next batch job, because the pipelines are already there.

Over time, everything runs on the same platform at the same rates, whether it needs to or not. It's a little like running a three-star kitchen and using it to make toast. The toast is fine. It's just very expensive toast.

## Check it yourself this week

For your 20 most expensive jobs, note:

- **Task types:** notebook, Python, JAR, SQL, DLT pipeline, ML.
- **Shape:** how many tasks, and how many dependencies between them.
- **Platform features in use:** Photon, serverless, GPUs, streaming.
- **Databricks-only code:** `dbutils`, Databricks SDK calls, or other APIs that only exist on Databricks.

You'll quickly see two groups. Jobs that use what makes Databricks special, and jobs that would run happily anywhere.

> The goal isn't to leave. It's to stop paying premium rates for work that doesn't need a premium platform.
