## The detail that trips people up

`list_prices` keeps the full price history for every SKU, so the join has to respect the window in which each price was valid. Join on SKU alone and every usage row is multiplied by every price that SKU has ever had. Here's the core of it:

```sql
SELECT u.usage_date, u.sku_name,
       u.workspace_id, u.usage_metadata.job_id,
       u.usage_quantity * p.pricing.effective_list.default AS list_cost
FROM system.billing.usage u
JOIN system.billing.list_prices p
  ON  u.sku_name   = p.sku_name
  AND u.cloud      = p.cloud
  AND u.usage_unit = p.usage_unit
  AND u.usage_start_time >= p.price_start_time
  AND (p.price_end_time IS NULL OR u.usage_start_time < p.price_end_time)
WHERE u.usage_date >= current_date() - INTERVAL 90 DAYS
```

Group that by `workspace_id` and `job_id` and you have your top jobs by cost (the empty `job_id` group is your untraced spend). Group by `sku_name` and you have the invoice, explained. These are list prices, before any contract discount.
