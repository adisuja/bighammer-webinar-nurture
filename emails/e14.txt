Here's a simple test you can apply to any savings proposal that lands on your desk, from us or from anyone else.


1. WHICH PARTS WERE MEASURED FROM YOUR OWN DATA?

Not benchmarked, not "typical for companies like you". Measured, from your billing and run history. If the answer is "none of it", you're reading a brochure.

2. WHICH PARTS WERE ASSUMED, AND AT WHAT RATE?

An estimate is fine. A hidden assumption isn't. Ask for the rate and the rule: "we assumed 40% of this cluster's time is idle because its p95 CPU is under 40%" is checkable. "Typically 30 to 50%" is not.

3. WHAT'S EXCLUDED?

Contract discounts, cloud VM costs and untraced spend change the picture. A savings figure at list price on a heavily discounted contract can be wildly optimistic.

4. COULD THE TOTAL SAVINGS EXCEED WHAT YOU ACTUALLY SPEND?

If the answer isn't an obvious no, something is double-counted. Moving a job to Jobs compute and then right-sizing it are not two savings on the same dollar. The second applies to what's left after the first.
