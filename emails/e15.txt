Somebody sized a cluster for a peak load months ago. Maybe during an incident, maybe for a one-off backfill. The peak passed. The cluster size didn't.

It still runs at that size every night.

WHY OVERSIZED CLUSTERS STAY OVERSIZED

Cluster size and autoscaling settings get set once and rarely revisited. Classic compute is billed on nodes and hours, not on how busy those nodes are. A cluster at 15% CPU costs the same as one at 90%.

The good news: Databricks already records what your clusters were doing, in system.compute.node_timeline.


WHY THE 95TH PERCENTILE MATTERS

An average can hide a spike. A cluster that averages 18% because of short bursts to 95% needs better autoscaling. A cluster that never goes above 30% needs a smaller size. Same average, different fix. The 95th percentile shows sustained load, and the maximum shows the spikes.

SELECT cluster_id,
       COUNT(DISTINCT date_trunc('minute', start_time)) AS observed_minutes,
       AVG(cpu_user_percent + cpu_system_percent)  AS avg_cpu,
       percentile_approx(cpu_user_percent + cpu_system_percent, 0.95) AS p95_cpu,
       MAX(cpu_user_percent + cpu_system_percent)  AS max_cpu
FROM system.compute.node_timeline
WHERE driver = false
  AND start_time >= current_date() - INTERVAL 30 DAYS
GROUP BY cluster_id
HAVING observed_minutes >= 30 AND avg_cpu < 20 AND p95_cpu < 40
ORDER BY avg_cpu

Read max_cpu alongside the others. A low p95 with a high max means short spikes: fix autoscaling. A low p95 and a low max means the cluster is simply too big: downsize. (Single-node clusters have no workers, so they won't appear. Serverless and SQL warehouses aren't in this table.)

CHECK IT YOURSELF THIS WEEK

- Clusters that ran 30 minutes or more, averaged under 20% CPU and stayed under 40% even at their 95th percentile. Those aren't busy, they're oversized.
- Clusters where autoscale minimum equals maximum, which means autoscaling is effectively off.
- Interactive clusters with no auto-termination set.

WHAT GOOD LOOKS LIKE

- Right-size from real utilisation, not from the size someone picked last year.
- Give autoscaling a real range.
- Auto-terminate interactive clusters.
- Review your top 10 clusters by cost every quarter.

Any dollar figure attached to right-sizing is an estimate, and should be labelled as one.
