Last time we added up what failed runs cost. Today, how to make that number shrink. The fixes are configuration, which is why this is usually the first win in any assessment.

1. Timeouts relative to normal runtime

Set each task's timeout from its real history, for example twice the median runtime. A stuck job then stops at 95 minutes instead of burning through to morning. Jobs with no timeout at all are the first ones to fix.

2. Retries with a ceiling

Retries are useful for flaky sources. They're expensive for bad code. Cap them (two is a sensible default), add an interval between attempts, and agree a clear rule for when a failure needs a human instead of another go.

3. Fail fast, before the expensive work

Put a cheap validation task at the front of the job: is the input there, is it the right shape, is it non-empty? Failing at minute one costs pennies. Failing at minute fifty costs the whole run.

4. Alerts that include the cost

"nightly_orders_etl failed" gets acknowledged. "nightly_orders_etl failed after two retries and burned $312" gets fixed. Put the cost and the owner in the alert, and failure stops being invisible to the people who pay for it.

The cheapest failure is the one that stops early and tells someone what it cost.
