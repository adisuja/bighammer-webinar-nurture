Every vendor in this space promises savings, and our own masterclass title said "up to 75%". So it's fair to ask what has to be true before any number like that deserves a hearing.

Most savings claims fall apart under three questions: what was actually measured, what was assumed, and what was left out.


WHY SAVINGS NUMBERS ARE SO OFTEN WRONG

- Measured and assumed get mixed together. A real failed-run cost sits next to a rough guess about right-sizing, and both are presented as "savings".
- The same dollar is counted twice. Moving a job to cheaper compute and right-sizing it are not two separate savings on the same spend.
- The gaps are hidden. Contract discounts, cloud VM costs and untraced spend quietly drop out of the picture.

HOW WE REPORT

Measured: total cost, cost by product, traced spend, failed-run cost. These come straight from your billing and run data.

Estimated: savings opportunities. Each one comes with the rule used to calculate it, so you can check it or change the assumption.

Visibility gap: untraced spend. Reported on its own line, never as savings.

WHAT GOOD REPORTING LOOKS LIKE

- Reliability waste (failed runs) kept separate from optimisation opportunity. They need different fixes and different owners.
- Every estimate shows its method.
- Nothing in the savings column that you couldn't explain to your CFO in one sentence.

> If you can't see the method, it isn't a number. It's a hope.
